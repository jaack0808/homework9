# Adam Smith, *An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations* (London, 1776), Book V, ch. I, Part III

- Type: PRIMARY (period commentary by a contemporary; 26 years after the window).
  Language: English.
- Fetched: https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/smith-adam/works/wealth-of-nations/book05/ch01c.htm

## Extracts (verbatim)

- "In 1743, the company advanced another million to government."
- The million was raised "not by a call upon the proprietors, but by selling annuities and
  contracting bond-debts."
- On 1730: competition had raised the price of goods in India so high "that they were not
  worth the buying" (the Company's argument against an open/regulated trade).
- Later dividends: the company "gradually augmented their dividend from about six to ten
  per cent" (before 1769); in 1773 it had to "reduce all at once their dividend to six per cent."
- General verdict on exclusive companies: "Without an exclusive privilege they have commonly
  mismanaged the trade. With an exclusive privilege they have both mismanaged and confined it."
- The Company was "altogether unfit to govern its territorial possessions."

## Used for

- Corroborates the 1743/44 £1m loan (raised by debt, not by a call on shareholders — i.e.,
  shareholders were not asked for new money).
- Contemporary critique of chartered monopolies for the ethics section; and the warning that
  the EIC's later profits came from territorial rule after 1757, i.e., after the window.
