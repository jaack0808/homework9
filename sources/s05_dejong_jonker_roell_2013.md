# Abe de Jong, Joost Jonker & Ailsa Röell, "Dutch Corporate Finance, 1602–1850", ERIM Report ERS-2013-008-F&A (2013); also in G. Caprio (ed.), *Handbook of Key Global Financial Markets, Institutions, and Infrastructure* (Elsevier, 2013), ch. 8

- Type: SECONDARY. Language: English.

## Extracts (p. 14; short quotes)

- VOC grew, "trebling the annual number of arriving ships and sales between 1670 and 1730".
- "Instead of facing reality and cutting high payouts to shareholders, the board continued them"
- "so the board was wedded to high dividends"; it "used mounting debt to plug the resulting
  budgetary holes".
- "investors suspected something and from 1732 the share price glided downwards."
- "From the end of the 1730s the VOC's annual cash flow regularly turned negative."
- "By 1760 debt had risen to 32.3 million guilders, overwhelmingly in short-term maturities."
- Liquidity crisis in the Fourth Anglo-Dutch War (1780–84); nationalised after 1795.

## Used for

Core of the VOC "fortunes" story: falling price from 1732, negative cash flow from late
1730s, dividends financed with debt.
