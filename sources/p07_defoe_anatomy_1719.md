# Daniel Defoe, *The Anatomy of Exchange-Alley: or, a System of Stock-Jobbing* (London: E. Smith, 1719)

- Type: PRIMARY (period pamphlet on the London stock market, published by "a Jobber").
  Language: English. Also reprinted as the appendix to Francis (1850).
- Fetched: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/ecco/004843169.0001.000/3:3 (ECCO-TCP keyed text, CC0)

## Extracts (verbatim, with page numbers of the 1719 edition)

- p. 3: "Coining false News, this way good, that way bad"
- p. 3: the trade is "a compleat System of Knavery", "founded in Fraud, born of Deceit, and
  nourished by Trick"
- p. 8: "Is not the whole Doctrine of Stock-Jobbing a Science of Fraud?"
- p. 13: "Letters have been order'd, by private Management" (forged letters from India about
  lost ships and wars, used to move East India stock)
- pp. 14–15: a jobber sells first to push the price down, then will "buy a Hundred Thousand
  Pound Stock, at 10 or 12 per Cent. under Price"
- p. 16: "run down true News as if it had been false, and run up false News as if it had been true"
- p. 17: the false report of the Pretender's capture "held us near a Fortnight in a firm
  Perswasion of the Thing"
- p. 24: stock-jobbing as practised is "neither less or more than High-Treason in its very Nature"

## Used for

"When to sell": East India stock moved on news and planted rumours, with 10–12% swings
engineered by jobbers. The letter should tell the ancestor to sell on *confirmed* events
(proclaimed peace, announced dividends), not on rumour.
