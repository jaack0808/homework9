## Extracts (short verbatim quotes; OCR errors kept)

- 1714–1720: "High dividends between 1714 and 1720 (each year 40 per cent) resulted in high
  prices; in the 'bubble' of 1720 a peak of 1240 per cent was reached!"
- 1725: "As prices on the stockmarket ran as high as 650 per cent during this year"
- Sept 1737, after news of wrecked return-fleet ships: "share prices feil [fell] from 620 to 580."
- Dividends "never lower than 12[½] per cent" (regular up to 1781).
- Shareholders' return: "a return on their investment of 2.9 per cent per year", hardly more
  than interest on state bonds.
- 1777 average price 373 per cent; prices low in the Seven Years' War and in 1773.
- Company debt cut to c. f7.6m in the early 18th c. and held there until c. 1780; bond
  interest fell to 3–2½% in the 18th c.; "anticipation" (short-term) money < f2m until 1735,
  then rose to a peak of f22m in 1765.
- Feb 1782 moratorium; taken over by the State in March 1796 with debts well over f100m.
- Many shareholders treated shares as family wealth and looked to the annual dividend.

## Used for

VOC purchase price in 1725 (650); 1737 level (580–620); dividend floor; 2.9% yield;
rising short-term borrowing after 1735; long-run collapse.
