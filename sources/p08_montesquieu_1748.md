## Extracts

- XV.1 (French): "Il n'est pas bon par sa nature; il n'est utile ni au maître ni à l'esclave"
  (of slavery). Common English: "The state of slavery is in its own nature bad."
- XV.5 (French, opening): "Si j'avais à soutenir le droit que nous avons eu de rendre les
  nègres esclaves, voici ce que je dirais :"
- XV.5 (translation): "Once the peoples of Europe had wiped out the people of America, they
  were obliged to enslave the peoples of Africa,"
- XV.5 (translation): "Sugar would be too expensive if there were no slaves to cultivate the
  plant it comes from." — an ironic "argument" Montesquieu puts in the mouth of slavery's defenders.
- XV.5 closing (translation, excerpt): "would it not have crossed the minds of the Princes of
  Europe, who together make so many pointless treatises,..." (i.e., to make one for mercy).
- Book XX (Amsterdam 1749 contents): ch. VI "Esprit de l'Angleterre sur le Commerce"; ch. VIII
  "De l'exclusion en fait de Commerce"; ch. X on companies — located but not quoted.

## Used for

Ethics section: the moral case against slavery was being made, in print, in exactly the year
the ancestor's window is open (1748). The ethical judgement in the paper is therefore not only
a modern standard imposed on the past.
