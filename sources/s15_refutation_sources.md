# Sources found in the refutation pass (searching for evidence against the draft)

## Niall Ferguson, *The Ascent of Money* (Penguin, 2008), ch. 3 — via Far Outliers excerpt
- Fetched: https://faroutliers.com/2012/03/13/the-financial-ascent-of-the-dutch-voc
- "Between 1602 and 1733, VOC stock rose from par (100) to an all-time peak of 786"
- "though its descent was more rapid, it still took more than sixty years to fall back down to
  120 in December 1794"
- Also: no VOC bubble in Ferguson's sense; 12.5% standard dividend set in 1632.
- Tension: Gaastra gives a 1720 bubble peak of 1240. Ferguson's "all-time peak" must exclude the
  1720 spike (likely annual data). GFD ("rallied to 800 in the 1730s") agrees with Ferguson.
- Effect on paper: VOC "rose, then declined" — the VOC's best exit was c. 1733, outside the
  1745–50 window. Doesn't change the recommendation.

## Søren Mentz, "Merchants and States: Private Trade and the Fall of Madras, 1746", *Journal of Indian Ocean World Studies* 2.1 (2018): 36–56
- Fetched: https://jiows.mcgill.ca/article/view/37/22
- "Trade with England was disrupted, private merchants lost their money, and the British state
  faced serious financial problems."
- Morse "promised to pay a ransom of 1,100,000 pagodas, or £481,250"; "The official ransom was
  never paid".
- Directors told shareholders Company losses were "modest, only £70,000"; when news arrived
  "the stock fell considerably."
- Effect: confirms 1746–47 as a bad time to sell EIC; Company-level loss modest relative to
  £3.2m capital, consistent with the unbroken 8% dividend.

## John Shovlin, "'No more victories! No more conquests!'", *Aspects of History* (based on *Trading with the Enemy*, Yale UP, 2021)
- Fetched: https://aspectsofhistory.com/the-east-india-companies-pull-back-from-empire/
- "War between the companies damaged trade and threatened their solvency in Europe."
- "Stock values fell, and shareholders mutinied, pushing the companies to reach an understanding."
- Dates: negotiations from June 1753 for nearly two years.
- Effect: supports selling by the end of 1750 rather than holding into the 1750s.
