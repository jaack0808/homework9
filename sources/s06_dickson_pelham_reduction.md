# P. G. M. Dickson, *The Financial Revolution in England: A Study in the Development of Public Credit, 1688–1756* (London: Macmillan, 1967), chapter on Pelham's reduction of interest (course excerpt)

- Type: SECONDARY. Language: English.
- Fetched: https://people.bu.edu/chamley/365-26W/Dickson.pdf (excerpt titled "Government
  Long-Term Borrowing"; excerpt pagination only).

## Extracts (short quotes)

- 3% stock "by the beginning of June 1749 had risen from 95 to just over par."
- Pelham in the Commons: "within three years the 3 per cents had risen gradually from 75,
  and now were at 101."
- 18 Aug 1749: Samson Gideon proposed to "reduce the company's bonds to 3% from 31 March 1750";
  directors agreed.
- EIC General Court ballot 3 Jan 1750: plan "carried against the directors by 296 votes to 209."
- 25 April 1750: the Company "capitulated — after acrimonious debate — and agreed to the
  government's terms."
- Act: "the whole of the £4,200,000 due from the state to the East India Company should be
  repaid to it in 1751" unless it agreed; if it agreed "it would be allowed to borrow up to
  £4.2m to discharge its bond debt."
- Terms: 4% debt to 3½% for a period, then 3%.

## Used for

Shape of the British market cycle: trough c. 1746–47 (3% stock at 75) to peak 1749–50
(above par) — the best time to sell a British company stock. Also the 1750 hit to EIC
income from the government debt (an argument for selling at the end of 1749 rather than
waiting — see notes/02).
