- Type: SECONDARY. Language: English. DOI 10.1017/S0968565010000338.

## Extracts (short quotes)

- "The threat of war generally caused a temporary fall in all British 'funds'"
- Actual war "created sharper and longer falls in government debt prices" (War of the
  Austrian Succession is the example given).
- "When peace negotiations signaled the end of the war, however, prices would rebound."
- The crisis "the result of the Jacobite incursion that year in the midst of the general
  European War of the Austrian Succession, caused all the British securities to dip sharply".
- "The surprising volatility of the English East India Company stock was clearly a matter of
  continuing concern for the Dutch investors."
- Post-war conversion: as the perpetual annuities rose toward par, "the government began a
  conversion" (Pelham, 1749–50).

## Used for

Timing: avoid selling during the Jacobite panic and the war; prices rebound with peace (1748).
Dutch investors held and traded EIC stock in Amsterdam (an ancestor could hold EIC from Amsterdam).
